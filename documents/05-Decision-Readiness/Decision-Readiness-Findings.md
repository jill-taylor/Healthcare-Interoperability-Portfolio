# Decision-Readiness Findings

## Heartland Integrated Health System (HIHS)
### Legacy Infusion Pump Modernization Assessment

**Assessment Phase:** Current-State Discovery and Decision Readiness  
**Project Type:** Assessment and Planning  
**Status:** Portfolio Artifact

---

## 1. Purpose

This document evaluates whether the current-state assessment has produced sufficient information to support informed decisions about the next phase of the Heartland Integrated Health System (HIHS) legacy infusion pump modernization effort.

The assessment is focused on decision readiness rather than a modernization recommendation. It considers whether the current environment, device population, clinical workflows, information flows, integration dependencies, cybersecurity considerations, lifecycle conditions, stakeholder perspectives, risks, and evidence gaps are sufficiently understood to support future planning.

This assessment does not establish a future-state architecture, select technology, recommend vendors, authorize procurement, or define implementation activities.

---

## 2. Decision-Readiness Approach

Decision readiness is evaluated by considering:

- Availability of current-state information
- Evidence supporting identified conditions and findings
- Understanding of clinical workflow dependencies
- Understanding of device connectivity and integration dependencies
- Visibility into lifecycle and vendor-support conditions
- Identification of cybersecurity and operational considerations
- Stakeholder participation and validation needs
- Known risks, gaps, assumptions, and dependencies
- Outstanding questions requiring further investigation

Decision readiness does not mean that all uncertainty has been eliminated. It means that the assessment has identified what is sufficiently understood, what remains uncertain, and what additional information is needed before subsequent decisions can be made responsibly.

---

## 3. Current-State Areas Sufficiently Understood

The assessment provides a structured current-state baseline across the major areas within scope.

### 3.1 Device Environment

The assessment establishes a preliminary inventory of approximately 480 infusion pumps across the identified HIHS facilities.

The inventory distinguishes device models, age, network connectivity, integration status, lifecycle/support conditions, and preliminary risk considerations.

The inventory provides a foundation for further validation and analysis.

### 3.2 Connectivity and Integration

The assessment identifies the major components and dependencies associated with the connected infusion-pump environment, including:

- Infusion pumps
- Network infrastructure
- Infusion integration gateway
- Enterprise interface engine
- EHR and supporting clinical systems
- Interface monitoring and alerting
- Network monitoring
- Identity and access management
- Clinical Engineering and IT asset records
- Vendor support

The assessment also identifies areas where interface protocols, transports, ownership, monitoring, and other technical details require confirmation.

### 3.3 Clinical Workflow

The assessment establishes the relationship between device use, clinical workflow, connectivity, information flow, lifecycle conditions, vendor support, and operational dependencies.

Additional validation is required before workflow conditions can be treated as fully established findings.

### 3.4 Cybersecurity and Operational Considerations

The assessment identifies cybersecurity considerations associated with device lifecycle, connectivity, access, monitoring, vendor dependencies, and operational continuity.

The assessment does not constitute a cybersecurity certification, formal compliance assessment, or security authorization.

### 3.5 Stakeholder and Ownership Considerations

The assessment identifies the major stakeholder groups and decision roles that would need to participate in subsequent planning.

Stakeholder input is distinguished from independently validated evidence where appropriate.

---

## 4. Evidence and Validation Status

The assessment distinguishes among:

- Confirmed or documented current-state information
- Stakeholder-reported information
- Portfolio assumptions
- Potential risks
- Evidence gaps
- Items requiring further investigation

This distinction is important because unresolved evidence gaps should not be treated as confirmed findings.

Several technical and operational details remain subject to validation, including device-specific interface characteristics, protocols and transports, ownership, monitoring, security controls, workflow variations, vendor conditions, and facility-specific differences.

---

## 5. Areas Requiring Further Investigation

The following areas should be investigated before major modernization, procurement, or implementation decisions are made.

### 5.1 Device and Lifecycle Validation

- Reconcile the device inventory with authoritative asset records.
- Confirm device age, lifecycle status, and vendor support conditions.
- Validate end-of-life and end-of-support information.
- Confirm differences among facilities and device populations.

### 5.2 Interface and Integration Validation

- Confirm actual source and destination systems.
- Confirm data exchanged and business purpose.
- Confirm protocols, message types, and transport mechanisms.
- Confirm interface ownership and operational support.
- Validate monitoring and failure-handling procedures.
- Identify facility-specific integration differences.

### 5.3 Clinical Workflow Validation

- Validate workflow observations with appropriate clinical stakeholders.
- Confirm how connectivity and information availability affect current workflows.
- Identify workflow variations across facilities.
- Validate downtime and continuity dependencies.

### 5.4 Cybersecurity Validation

- Confirm current security controls and monitoring capabilities.
- Validate device and network visibility.
- Confirm access and identity-management dependencies.
- Determine the extent of vendor involvement in device support and security.
- Further investigate unresolved cybersecurity concerns.

### 5.5 Evidence and Documentation

- Identify missing or incomplete technical documentation.
- Reconcile conflicting information.
- Identify unresolved assumptions.
- Establish authoritative sources for information required for subsequent planning.

---

## 6. Decisions Supported by the Current Assessment

The assessment provides sufficient structure and information to support decisions about **what requires additional investigation and what information should be collected during the next phase**.

The assessment can also support discussion of:

- Current-state information gaps
- Areas of elevated attention
- Stakeholder and ownership dependencies
- Technical and clinical validation needs
- Additional evidence required for future planning
- Whether additional discovery is necessary before proceeding

These findings support planning for the next phase but do not constitute authorization to implement a modernization solution.

---

## 7. Decisions Not Yet Supported

The current assessment does not provide sufficient evidence to support:

- Selection of a specific replacement device or technology
- Vendor selection
- Procurement decisions
- Final future-state architecture
- Production integration design
- Implementation planning at the technical execution level
- Final cybersecurity design or authorization
- Formal clinical safety determination
- Enterprise risk acceptance
- Final modernization sequencing or investment decisions

These decisions require additional evidence, validation, analysis, and appropriate technical, clinical, operational, cybersecurity, financial, and leadership review.

---

## 8. Decision-Readiness Summary

The HIHS assessment has established a structured understanding of the current legacy infusion-pump environment and identified the major clinical, technical, operational, lifecycle, integration, cybersecurity, and stakeholder dependencies affecting future modernization planning.

The assessment also identifies meaningful evidence gaps and areas requiring further validation.

**Overall decision-readiness determination:**

> **Current-state discovery is sufficiently developed to identify next-phase information and validation needs, but additional investigation is required before major modernization, procurement, architecture, or implementation decisions can be responsibly made.**

This determination reflects the discovery-only scope of the assessment and the principle of understanding the current environment before recommending a future-state solution.

---

## 9. Decision-Readiness Considerations for the Next Phase

Before proceeding to future-state planning, HIHS should consider whether the following conditions have been adequately addressed:

- Device inventory has been validated against authoritative records.
- Lifecycle and vendor-support information has been confirmed.
- Critical interfaces and information flows have been validated.
- Clinical workflow dependencies have been confirmed with appropriate stakeholders.
- Cybersecurity and operational concerns have been further investigated.
- Facility-specific differences have been identified.
- Major evidence gaps and unresolved assumptions have been addressed or explicitly documented.
- Appropriate stakeholders are prepared to participate in subsequent planning and decision-making.

The next phase should build on the evidence collected during this assessment rather than assume that unresolved conditions have been resolved.

---

## 10. Assessment Outcome

The Decision-Readiness Findings provide a transition point between current-state discovery and future planning.

The assessment does not recommend a modernization solution. Instead, it identifies what is sufficiently understood, what remains uncertain, and what additional evidence is required to support responsible future decisions.

This supports the HIHS principle:

> **Understand Before Recommending.**

---

## 11. Scope and Limitations

This document represents a fictional portfolio assessment for Heartland Integrated Health System.

The assessment is limited to current-state discovery and decision readiness.

It does not constitute:

- A production healthcare assessment
- A clinical safety determination
- A cybersecurity certification or authorization
- A regulatory or compliance determination
- A formal enterprise risk assessment
- A vendor evaluation
- A procurement recommendation
- A future-state architecture
- An implementation plan
- A production configuration

Where evidence is incomplete, the assessment identifies the condition as requiring further investigation rather than presenting an unsupported conclusion.
